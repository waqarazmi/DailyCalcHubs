Everything You Need to Know About the Saudi Dependent Levy

The Expatriate Dependent Levy (known in Saudi administrative terminology as Rasm Al-Murafiqeen - رسم المرافقين) is a state surcharge levied on family members sponsored by foreign workers residing in the Kingdom. First phased in during July 2017 at 100 SAR per month, the levy reached its permanent statutory rate of 400 SAR per month per dependent (4,800 SAR per year) in July 2020 and remains at this rate for 2026.

Who is Classified as a Dependent?

Under Jawazat immigration guidelines, the 400 SAR monthly surcharge applies to:

  • Spouse: The legal wife of the expatriate employee.
  • Children: Unmarried daughters and minor sons sponsored under the father's Iqama.
  • Sponsored Parents / In-Laws: Dependent parents sponsored under exceptional family visas.

Dependent Levy vs. Exit-ReEntry Visa Charges

It is essential to distinguish between the statutory dependent levy and travel visa charges:

  • Statutory Dependent Levy: A mandatory government fee of 400 SAR per month per individual, paid to Jawazat through SADAD Alien Control (Code 092) to maintain lawful residence.
  • Exit-ReEntry Visa Surcharge: A separate travel authorization fee (200 SAR for a single trip up to 2 months; 500 SAR for a multiple-trip visa up to 3 months). Jawazat rules strictly require that all dependent levies through the expiration date of the sponsor's Iqama must be settled in full before any exit-reentry visa can be issued.
Government Fees vs. Private Medical Insurance: The 400 SAR monthly dependent levy is exclusively a government residency fee. It does not include private medical insurance, which must be purchased separately through CHI-accredited insurance providers. Frequently Asked Questions

Are any categories exempt from paying the dependent fee?
Yes. Exempt categories include non-Saudi wives of Saudi citizens, non-Saudi children of Saudi mothers, domestic workers (drivers/housemaids), and citizens of Gulf Cooperation Council (GCC) countries.
How are dependent fees calculated for a newborn baby born in Saudi Arabia?
When adding a newborn baby to an Iqama, the dependent fee is prorated from the baby's exact date of birth until the expiration date of the father's current Iqama at the standard rate of 400 SAR per month.
Can dependent fees be refunded if a family leaves permanently on Final Exit?
Under Saudi Ministry of Finance regulations, dependent fees paid through SADAD are non-refundable once the residency period has commenced, even if dependents depart the Kingdom permanently before the end of the paid validity period.
