Complete Framework: Saudi Iqama Renewal & Expat Levies in 2026

Maintaining valid legal residency in the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia is essential for all expatriate employees and their accompanying family members. Over recent years, the Saudi government has modernized its immigration and labor frameworks under Vision 2030, introducing flexible quarterly renewals via the Qiwa, Absher, and Muqeem platforms.

1. Statutory Jawazat Fee (Civil Passport Authority)

The base residency fee collected by the General Directorate of Passports (Jawazat) through the Ministry of Interior SADAD billing system remains standardized:

  • Commercial / Private Sector Worker: 650 SAR per year (inclusive of HRSD administrative surcharge). Under the quarterly scheme, this is prorated to 162.50 SAR for 3 months, 325 SAR for 6 months, and 487.50 SAR for 9 months.
  • Domestic Worker (Amil Manzili, Private Driver, Housemaid): 600 SAR per year for up to 4 domestic workers sponsored by a Saudi national. Additional domestic workers beyond statutory caps incur the domestic worker levy.
  • Spouse / Child Over 18: Separate residency cards may incur standard 500 SAR civil passport issuance fees.

2. Maktab Amal Financial Levy (Ministry of Human Resources)

The financial expatriate work permit surcharge (Rasm Maktab Amal) is levied on commercial entities to encourage national employment under the Nitaqat Saudization system:

  • Tier A (Expats exceed Saudis): 800 SAR per month (9,600 SAR annually per expatriate employee).
  • Tier B (Saudis equal or exceed Expats): 700 SAR per month (8,400 SAR annually per expatriate employee).
Important Legal Notice (Article 40):
Under Article 40(1) of the Saudi Labor Law, the employer is legally obligated to bear the full cost of the employee's Iqama, work permit (Maktab Amal), and renewal fees. Passing these fees onto the worker or deducting them from monthly salaries is a direct violation of MHRSD regulations.

3. Expat Dependent Surcharge (Rasm Al-Murafiqeen)

Introduced in July 2017, the Expat Dependent Levy is fixed at 400 SAR per month per dependent (4,800 SAR per year per family member). This applies to spouses, sons, daughters, and sponsored parents. Unlike Maktab Amal, the dependent levy is the personal financial responsibility of the expatriate employee and must be settled prior to Iqama renewal or the issuance of exit-reentry visas.

Frequently Asked Questions

What happens if an Iqama expires without timely renewal?
Under Jawazat regulations, if an Iqama is not renewed within three (3) days of its expiration date, a delay penalty of 500 SAR is imposed for the first offense. For a second offense, the penalty doubles to 1,000 SAR. A third consecutive offense may result in deportation under Saudi residency laws.
Can I pay dependent fees in quarterly installments?
Yes. If your employer chooses to renew your work permit and Iqama for 3, 6, or 9 months under Cabinet Decision 515, the dependent levy can also be paid for that matching duration (e.g. 1,200 SAR per dependent for 3 months) through the SADAD government payment system.
Is health insurance mandatory before Iqama renewal?
Yes. The Council of Health Insurance (CHI) electronically connects all certified Saudi health insurers to the Jawazat immigration database. If the employee or any dependent does not have an active medical insurance policy registered, Absher and Muqeem will block the renewal transaction.
