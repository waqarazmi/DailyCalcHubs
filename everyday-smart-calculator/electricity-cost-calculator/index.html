How Household Electricity Bills are Calculated

Electric utility companies bill consumers based on kilowatt-hours (kWh), which represent the amount of energy transferred over time. One kilowatt-hour equals 1,000 watts of electrical power sustained continuously for one hour.

Mathematically Valid Electricity Formulas

Daily Energy (kWh) = [Power (Watts) ÷ 1,000] × Hours Used Per Day
Billing Period Energy = Daily Energy (kWh) × Number of Days
Estimated Cost = Billing Period Energy (kWh) × Tariff Rate per kWh

Worked Calculation Example

Consider a standard 1.5-ton split air conditioner running for 8 hours per day during the summer in Riyadh, under the Saudi SEC residential Tier 1 tariff of 0.18 SAR/kWh:

  • Step 1 (Convert watts to kW): 1,800 Watts ÷ 1,000 = 1.8 kW.
  • Step 2 (Determine daily consumption): 1.8 kW × 8 hours = 14.4 kWh per day.
  • Step 3 (Calculate 30-day monthly consumption): 14.4 kWh × 30 days = 432 kWh per month.
  • Step 4 (Multiply by tariff rate): 432 kWh × 0.18 SAR = 77.76 SAR per month.

Utility Billing Variables Disclaimer

Calculated figures represent mathematical estimates of appliance consumption. Actual utility bills vary depending on progressive tiered tariff brackets (e.g. SEC higher rate tier above 6,000 kWh), fixed meter service fees, air conditioner compressor duty cycle efficiency, seasonal peak rates, and statutory taxes (such as 15% ZATCA VAT in Saudi Arabia). Verify your invoice against your official utility provider's billing statement.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do you calculate kilowatt-hours (kWh) from appliance wattage?
Multiply the appliance wattage by daily hours of operation and divide by 1,000: kWh = (Watts × Hours) ÷ 1,000.
What is the electricity tariff in Saudi Arabia?
The Saudi Electricity Company (SEC) residential tariff traditionally sets 0.18 SAR/kWh for the first 1 to 6,000 kWh per month, and 0.30 SAR/kWh for consumption above 6,000 kWh. Consumers can customize their tariff directly in the calculator to reflect their specific rate or commercial class.
Does a refrigerator run at its full wattage 24 hours a day?
No. Modern refrigerators cycle their cooling compressor on and off according to internal thermostat settings, typically running at full compressor power between 30% and 50% of the total day.
