How Grade Point Average (GPA) is Calculated

Grade Point Average (GPA) is the universal metric used by secondary schools, colleges, and universities to summarize a student's overall academic performance. Rather than taking a simple unweighted average of letter grades, collegiate GPA systems weight each course by its assigned credit hours (semester hours), ensuring that intensive courses exert an appropriately proportionate impact.

Mathematically Valid Weighted GPA Formula

Course Grade Points = Letter Grade Numerical Equivalent × Course Credit Hours
Semester GPA = Sum of All Course Grade Points ÷ Total Semester Credit Hours
Cumulative GPA = [(Prior Cumulative GPA × Prior Credits) + Current Semester Points] ÷ (Prior Credits + Current Credits)

Academic Standards Disclaimer

Grading standards, grade point weightings, and honors thresholds vary across academic institutions, faculties, and ministries of education. While this tool models the most prevalent standard 4.0 and Saudi 5.0 systems, students should verify their specific degree requirements against their official university registrar handbook.

Frequently Asked Questions

How does credit hour weighting affect GPA?
Credit hours represent course weight. An "A" in a 4-credit course yields 16 grade points (on a 4.0 scale), whereas an "A" in a 1-credit lab produces 4 grade points. The total grade points are divided by total credits.
Why do Saudi universities use a 5.0 GPA scale?
Public universities in Saudi Arabia (such as King Saud University, King Abdulaziz University, and KFUPM) follow a 5.0 grading scale established by the Ministry of Education to provide granular distinction between A+ (5.00), A (4.75), and B+ (4.50) levels of achievement.
How do I calculate cumulative GPA?
Enter your previous cumulative GPA and previous completed credits in the optional cumulative box. The calculator mathematically merges your past credits with your current semester grades to produce your updated cumulative standing.
