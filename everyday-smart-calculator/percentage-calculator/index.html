How Every Common Percentage Calculation Works

The term percent originates from the Latin per centum, meaning "by the hundred." It represents a mathematical fraction or ratio with a denominator of 100. Whether calculating retail sales tax, financial growth rates, academic grade scores, or recipe measurements, understanding core percentage formulas simplifies everyday decision-making.

Mathematically Valid Percentage Formulas

1. Value of a Percentage: Result = (P ÷ 100) × Total
2. Ratio as a Percentage: Percentage = (Part ÷ Whole) × 100
3. Percentage Change: % Change = [(New Value - Old Value) ÷ |Old Value|] × 100
4. Value After Percentage Increase: Result = Base × (1 + [P ÷ 100])
5. Value After Percentage Decrease: Result = Base × (1 - [P ÷ 100])

Worked Calculation Examples

  • Example 1 (Retail VAT): Finding 15% Saudi VAT on a 400 SAR purchase: (15 ÷ 100) × 400 = 60 SAR.
  • Example 2 (Test Score Ratio): Scoring 36 correct answers out of 45 questions: (36 ÷ 45) × 100 = 80.00%.
  • Example 3 (Salary Raise): An employee earning 8,000 SAR per month receives a 1,200 SAR increase to 9,200 SAR: [(9,200 - 8,000) ÷ 8,000] × 100 = +15.00% growth.

Percentage Change vs. Percentage Difference

A frequent point of confusion is the distinction between percentage change and percentage difference:

  • Percentage Change: Applied when there is a clear chronological sequence from an old starting state to a new ending state (e.g. quarterly revenue growth, inflation increase, weight loss).
  • Percentage Difference: Applied when comparing two contemporaneous values without an implied before-or-after direction (e.g. comparing the price of gasoline in two neighboring cities).

Arithmetic Accuracy Notice

Calculations are computed using 64-bit IEEE floating-point arithmetic. Division by zero is mathematically undefined; enter non-zero denominators for ratio and change calculations.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do you calculate what percentage one number is of another?
Divide the partial number by the total whole number, and multiply by 100: Percentage = (Part ÷ Whole) × 100.
How do you calculate percentage increase or decrease?
Subtract the old value from the new value, divide the result by the absolute value of the old value, and multiply by 100: % Change = [(New - Old) ÷ |Old|] × 100. A positive result denotes an increase; a negative result denotes a decrease.
Can percentage calculations handle negative numbers?
Yes. The calculator fully supports negative numbers, such as calculating percentage drops in temperature, financial losses, or negative baseline adjustments.
