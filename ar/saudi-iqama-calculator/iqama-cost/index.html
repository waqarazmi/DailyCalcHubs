The Council of Health Insurance requires private sector employers to provide comprehensive healthcare coverage for workers and their eligible family dependents:

  • Worker policy must be active before renewal
  • Co-pay maximum capped at 20% by CCHI
  • Minimum policy benefits cover emergency care

Understanding the True Cost of Residency in Saudi Arabia

Calculating the true cost of an expatriate's residency in Saudi Arabia involves more than the basic passport stamp fee. A complete financial projection must capture the interaction between government statutory fees, MHRSD work permit surcharges, مجلس الضمان الصحي (CHI) medical insurance premiums, and dependent family levies.

Employer Cost-to-Company (CTC)

Under Article 40(1) of the Saudi Labor Law, the employer must bear all costs associated with bringing the worker into the Kingdom, renewing their residency permit (Jawazat), paying their annual work permit (Maktab Amal), and providing mandatory private health insurance. These items constitute the statutory Employer CTC.

Employee Personal Share & Indicative Healthcare Estimates

In contrast to the employer's statutory dues, accompanying family dependent surcharges (400 SAR per month per dependent) are personal employee obligations under SADAD Code 092. Medical insurance premiums (modeled at an indicative estimate such as 800 SAR per individual per year) represent private-market estimates based on accredited commercial health insurance tiers, not government statutory charges. Actual medical premiums vary by insurer, policy tier, age, and health underwriting.

الأسئلة الشائعة

Can health insurance be renewed quarterly alongside 3-month Iqama?
Generally no. Most accredited health insurance providers in Saudi Arabia issue medical insurance policies on an annual 12-month basis. While Iqama and work permits can be renewed quarterly, the underlying medical insurance policy must be valid for the duration of the renewal period.
Are employers legally allowed to deduct worker health insurance from salaries?
No. Under the Cooperative Health Insurance Law and Article 40 of Saudi Labor Law, employers are strictly required to provide and fund basic cooperative health insurance for their employees without making salary deductions.
Who pays the dependent levy if the employer provides a family visa?
Under Saudi regulations, the employee is legally responsible for paying the 400 SAR per month dependent levy through SADAD Code 092 unless their employment contract explicitly stipulates that the company covers dependent surcharges as part of an executive benefits package.
