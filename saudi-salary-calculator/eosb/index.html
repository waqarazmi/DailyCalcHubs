Complete Guide to End of Service Benefits (EOSB) in Saudi Arabia

The End of Service Benefit (EOSB) — known in Arabic as Mukaafat Nihayat Al-Khidma (Ù…ÙƒØ§ÙØ£Ø© Ù†Ù‡Ø§ÙŠØ© Ø§Ù„Ø®Ø¯Ù…Ø©) — is a mandatory severance entitlement guaranteed under the Saudi Labor Law promulgated by Royal Decree No. M/51. Governed primarily by Articles 84, 85, 87, and 88, EOSB serves as the legal financial cushion paid by the employer upon completion or termination of an employment relationship.

The Wage Baseline Used for EOSB

One of the most frequent legal disputes before Saudi Labor Tribunals revolves around which salary figure should be used. Article 2 of the Labor Law explicitly defines "Wage" as the basic wage plus all allowances and increments payable to the worker in return for their labor.

Binding Judicial Benchmark:
EOSB is calculated on the employee's last drawn comprehensive wage (Basic Salary + Housing Allowance + Transport Allowance + Fixed Monthly Allowances). Discretionary annual bonuses and reimbursable business expenses are excluded.

How Fractions of Service are Prorated

Under Article 84, service is not rounded down to whole years. If an employee has worked for 4 years, 7 months, and 12 days, the payout incorporates the exact pro-rata daily fraction of the statutory entitlement, ensuring zero loss of earned gratuity.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can an employer forfeit EOSB under Article 80?
Yes. Under Article 80 of the Saudi Labor Law, an employer may terminate an employee without notice and without EOSB award only under very strict circumstances (e.g. assault on the employer, forgery, intentional property damage, or breach of industrial trade secrets), provided due disciplinary investigation and notification protocols were observed.
What is the payment deadline for EOSB under Article 88?
Under Article 88, if the employer terminates the worker, all wages and EOSB must be paid within one (1) week. If the worker resigns, the employer must settle all dues within two (2) weeks.
