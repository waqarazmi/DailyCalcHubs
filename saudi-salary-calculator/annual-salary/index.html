How Annual Compensation Packages Work in Saudi Arabia

Navigating job offers and employment compensation packages in the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia requires understanding the distinction between your Monthly Take-Home Salary, your Annual Gross Package, and your employer’s Total Cost to Company (CTC). Unlike many Western jurisdictions that quote annual lump-sum salaries with income tax withholdings, Saudi Arabia features 0% personal income tax on salaries, making allowance structures and social insurance contributions the primary determinants of net earnings.

Anatomy of a Standard Saudi Offer Letter

In Saudi private sector contracts validated on Qiwa, compensation packages are generally divided into three explicit components:

  • Basic Salary: Represents the primary legal baseline for labor entitlements under the Saudi Labor Law. It directly determines End of Service Gratuity (EOSB) under Articles 84-85.
  • Housing Allowance: In the majority of Saudi firms, housing allowance is structured as either 25% of the basic salary or a fixed minimum equivalent to 3 months' basic salary per calendar year.
  • Transportation Allowance: Typically budgeted at 10% of the basic salary to cover daily work commuting expenses.
Important GOSI Contributory Rule:
Only Basic Salary and Housing Allowance are combined to form the Contributory Wage subject to General Organization for Social Insurance deductions (capped at 45,000 SAR/month). Transport, mobile phone, and annual bonus allowances are legally exempt from GOSI deductions.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is personal income tax deducted from annual salaries in Saudi Arabia?
No. There is 0% personal income tax on employment salaries for both Saudi citizens and expatriate employees in the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia. Expatriates receive 100% of their gross agreed wage without income tax deductions.
What is included in Total Cost to Company (CTC)?
CTC encompasses the employee's total annual gross salary (Basic + Housing + Transport + Allowances + Bonuses), plus the employer's mandatory GOSI contributions (11.75% for Saudis, 2% for Expats), private health insurance premiums, annual flight ticket allowances, and government residency fees (Iqama renewal, work permits) for expatriates.
