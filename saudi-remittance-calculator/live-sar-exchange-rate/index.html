Understanding the Saudi Riyal (SAR) Exchange Rate

The Saudi Riyal is the sovereign currency of the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia, issued and administered by the Saudi Central Bank (SAMA). For nearly four decades, the Riyal has maintained one of the world's most stable monetary anchors through its fixed peg to the United States Dollar.

The SAMA Fixed US Dollar Peg (1 USD = 3.75 SAR)

In June 1986, SAMA formally pegged the Saudi Riyal to the US Dollar at exactly 1 USD = 3.75 SAR. Because Saudi Arabia's primary export (crude oil and petrochemicals) is invoiced globally in US Dollars, the peg protects the national budget from exchange volatility, eliminates currency risk for international trade partners, and ensures price stability for consumer goods imported into the Kingdom.

How Other Currency Rates (INR, PKR, BDT, PHP) Are Derived

Because SAR is pegged to USD, the exchange rate of the Saudi Riyal against any other currency is calculated via cross-currency mathematics:

1 SAR in Target Currency = (Target Currency per USD) ÷ 3.75

When the Indian Rupee or Pakistani Rupee depreciates against the US Dollar on global exchanges, the Saudi Riyal automatically appreciates against those currencies by the exact same proportion!

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is the Saudi Riyal (SAR) pegged to the US Dollar?
The Saudi Central Bank (SAMA) has pegged the Saudi Riyal to the US Dollar at a fixed rate of 1 USD = 3.75 SAR since June 1986. This provides monetary stability, shields oil revenues from currency volatility, and prevents import inflation.
