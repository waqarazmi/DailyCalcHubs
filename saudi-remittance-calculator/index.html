How International Remittances Work in Saudi Arabia

The Kingdom of Saudi Arabia is among the world's highest outbound remittance corridors, serving millions of expatriates from South Asia, Southeast Asia, and the Middle East. Remittance operations in the Kingdom are regulated by the Saudi Central Bank (SAMA) to safeguard consumer capital and guarantee strict anti-money laundering (AML) controls.

1. The Difference Between Interbank Rates and Retail App Rates

When you search currency conversions on major search engines, you see the interbank spot rate. This is the institutional rate at which multi-billion-dollar sovereign funds and global financial institutions exchange foreign currencies.

Licensed consumer digital wallets (such as Barq, Urpay, and STC Pay) and bank exchange centers (such as Tahweel Al Rajhi and Enjaz) apply a retail exchange rate spread. The spread represents the difference between the wholesale price and the retail price offered to consumers. It covers FX settlement overhead, foreign banking counterparty fees, and currency fluctuation risk.

Key Formula for Remittance Payout:
Net Foreign Currency Received = (Transfer Amount in SAR - Transfer Fee in SAR) × Retail Exchange Rate
Always check both the fee AND the exchange rate: an app advertising "Zero Fee" may have a slightly wider exchange rate spread, whereas an app with a 15 SAR fee may offer an exchange rate that yields higher total payout on large transfers!

2. Value Added Tax (VAT) on Remittances in Saudi Arabia

Under the directives of the Zakat, Tax and Customs Authority (ZATCA), financial transfers themselves are exempt from VAT. The 15% VAT is applied strictly to the service fee charged by the remittance provider.

  • Zero Fee Promotion (e.g. Barq): Total VAT charged = 0.00 SAR.
  • Standard Wallet Transfer (10.00 SAR fee): 10.00 SAR + 1.50 SAR VAT = 11.50 SAR total deduction.
  • Bank Counter / SWIFT (15.00 SAR fee): 15.00 SAR + 2.25 SAR VAT = 17.25 SAR total deduction.

3. SAMA Regulations & Transfer Limit Monitoring

To ensure compliance with Saudi financial regulations:

  • Your remittance digital wallet must be registered using your valid Iqama (Civil ID) and authenticated via the Nafath National Single Sign-On platform.
  • Financial institutions monitor transfer volumes in accordance with declared income and KYC profiles under Saudi Central Bank (SAMA) anti-money laundering regulations.
  • For high-value transfers exceeding standard limits, financial institutions may request supplementary documentation, such as end-of-service settlement receipts or asset sale declarations.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which app gives the best rate from Saudi Arabia to India?
Digital remittance apps like Barq frequently provide the highest in-hand payout for India because they offer a tight spread (typically 0.25 to 0.30 INR below spot) combined with a zero-transfer-fee model. Urpay and STC Pay are also highly competitive, especially for transactions over 3,000 SAR.
How long does it take for money sent from Saudi Arabia to arrive?
Most modern digital wallets utilize instant clearing networks (such as India's IMPS, Pakistan's Raast, Bangladesh's BEFTN/NPSB, and the Philippines' GCash/InstaPay). Funds sent via these digital channels typically credit within 2 to 10 minutes. Traditional SWIFT bank wire transfers take 1 to 2 business days.
Is there any tax on money sent from Saudi Arabia to home countries?
In Saudi Arabia, there is zero income tax or remittance capital tax. In destination countries like India and Pakistan, money sent to non-resident bank accounts (NRE) or to close dependent relatives (parents, spouse, children) is completely tax-free under double taxation avoidance frameworks and gift tax exemptions.
Can I send money from Saudi Arabia using an expired Iqama?
No. Under SAMA directives, financial institutions and digital wallets automatically freeze international debit transactions as soon as an expat's Iqama expires. You must renew your Iqama and re-authenticate via Absher/Nafath before international remittance channels are reactivated.
