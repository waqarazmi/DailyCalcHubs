Saudi to India Remittance Complete Guide

The authoritative handbook for Indian expatriates in the Kingdom. Everything you need to know about NRE vs NRO accounts, Indian Income Tax exemptions, Tax Collected at Source (TCS), and SAMA compliance.

1. Overview of the Saudi-India Remittance Corridor

Over 2.5 million Indian citizens live and work across the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia, representing the largest expatriate workforce in the country. From corporate executives and engineers in Riyadh to healthcare professionals in Jeddah and industrial technicians in Jubail, Indian expats send billions of Saudi Riyals back to India annually.

Recent advancements in digital banking regulated by the Saudi Central Bank (SAMA) have transformed remittances. Transfers that previously took days at crowded exchange houses can now be executed in seconds from mobile smartphones with zero upfront fees.

2. Indian Income Tax & Legal Regulations

A frequent question asked by expats is whether money transferred from Saudi Arabia to India will be taxed by the Indian Income Tax Department. Here is the definitive legal position under Indian tax law:

Foreign Earnings Are 100% Tax Free in India

Under Section 5 and Section 6 of the Indian Income Tax Act 1961, if you qualify as a Non-Resident Indian (NRI)—generally defined as spending 182 days or more outside India during the financial year—income earned outside India is completely exempt from Indian income tax. Because Saudi Arabia has zero personal income tax, your Saudi earnings remain completely tax-free in both countries!

Transfers to Relatives Are Gift-Tax Exempt

Under Section 56(2)(x) of the Income Tax Act, any sum of money received from a defined "relative" is completely exempt from gift tax with no upper financial ceiling. Defined exempt relatives include:

  • Spouse
  • Parents and Grandparents
  • Children and Grandchildren
  • Brothers and Sisters (including their spouses)

Money sent to your parents or spouse for family maintenance or medical care is 100% tax-free in their hands.

TCS (Tax Collected at Source) Does NOT Apply

There is widespread confusion regarding India's 20% Tax Collected at Source (TCS) rules. TCS applies exclusively to money remitted OUT of India under the Reserve Bank of India's Liberalised Remittance Scheme (LRS). Inward remittances transferred into India from Saudi Arabia attract zero TCS.

3. NRE vs NRO Accounts: Which Should You Use?

Every Indian expatriate in Saudi Arabia should maintain designated non-resident bank accounts:

Feature NRE (Non-Resident External) NRO (Non-Resident Ordinary)
Primary Purpose Depositing foreign Saudi earnings Managing income earned inside India
Eligible Credits Inward remittances from SAR only Indian rent, dividends, pension, local transfers
Tax on Interest 100% Tax-Free in India Subject to 30% TDS (+ cess)
Repatriability Freely repatriable back to Saudi Arabia Repatriable up to USD 1 Million/year (Form 15CA/CB)
Currency Conversion Held in Indian Rupees (INR) Held in Indian Rupees (INR)
Expert Expat Tip:
Always direct your Saudi remittances into your NRE savings or NRE Fixed Deposit (FD) account. Because interest earned on NRE deposits is 100% exempt from Indian income tax, you earn high Indian interest rates completely tax-free!

4. SAMA Regulations & Transfer Limits in Saudi Arabia

The Saudi Central Bank (SAMA) requires licensed remittance apps to enforce anti-money laundering (AML) controls:

  • Salary Matching: Your annual international transfers are cross-referenced with your verified monthly salary registered with GOSI.
  • Valid Iqama Required: International transfers are immediately halted if your Iqama expires until renewed and re-authenticated via Absher/Nafath.
  • Large Transactions: If remitting a sum larger than your regular monthly wage (e.g. End of Service gratuity settlement or asset liquidation), keep your official settlement papers or employer letter handy for bank verification.

5. How to Send Money to India in 5 Minutes

  1. Download an approved licensed app (such as Barq, urpay, or STC Pay).
  2. Complete instant biometric verification via the Nafath National Single Sign-On.
  3. Top up your Saudi wallet using your Mada debit card or direct bank transfer.
  4. Add your Indian beneficiary details: Account Number, Account Type (NRE or Savings), and IFSC Code.
  5. Confirm transfer amount, verify locked exchange rate, and tap send. Funds credit instantly via IMPS!

Frequently Asked Questions

Is money sent from Saudi Arabia to India taxable under Indian law?
No. Non-Resident Indian (NRI) earnings abroad are completely tax-free in India. Inward remittances transferred into NRE bank accounts or sent to close relatives (parents, spouse, siblings, children) are 100% tax-exempt under Section 56(2)(x) of the Income Tax Act.
Does Tax Collected at Source (TCS) apply to money sent from Saudi to India?
No. TCS applies strictly to outbound remittances sent FROM India under the Liberalised Remittance Scheme (LRS). Inward remittances sent TO India from Saudi Arabia attract zero TCS.
What is the difference between NRE and NRO bank accounts for Saudi NRIs?
An NRE (Non-Resident External) account is used for depositing tax-free foreign currency remittances from Saudi Arabia, and interest earned is 100% tax-free. An NRO (Non-Resident Ordinary) account is used for managing income earned in India (such as rental income, dividends) and interest is subject to TDS.
