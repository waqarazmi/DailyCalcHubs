GCC & International Driving License Regulations

Operating a rental vehicle across the GCC region requires compliance with specific licensing frameworks:

  • Saudi Arabia (KSA): Tourists holding a valid visit or Umrah visa may legally drive using their domestic driver's license alongside an International Driving Permit (IDP) for up to 3 months. GCC national citizens (UAE, Kuwait, Qatar, Bahrain, Oman) may drive on their national licenses directly. Resident expatriates on an Iqama must hold an official Saudi Moroor driving license.
  • United Arab Emirates (UAE): Tourists from 50+ recognized countries (including the US, UK, Canada, Australia, EU, and GCC nations) can rent vehicles directly using their national domestic license. Visitors from other nations require an International Driving Permit (IDP) accompanied by their original license.
  • Europe & North America: For non-EU license holders renting cars in Europe, an IDP is strictly required by rental agencies in Italy, Spain, France, and Austria to satisfy local traffic enforcement rules.

Security Deposits, Insurance & Fuel Policies

To avoid unexpected deductions, ensure you understand key rental contract components:

Policy Component Standard Terms DailyCalcHubs Advice
Security Deposit Credit card pre-authorization between SAR/AED 1,000 to 3,500. Ensure your credit card has sufficient available credit. Debit cards are rarely accepted for deposits. Holds are released within 14-28 days.
Collision Damage Waiver (CDW) Basic insurance covers damage with an excess/deductible of SAR 1,500 - 3,000. Choose "Full Protection" or Zero-Excess coverage to eliminate financial liability for windshield, tires, and body scrapes.
Fuel Policy Full-to-Full: Vehicle delivered with a full tank and must be returned full. Always refill near the return depot. Rental agency refueling charges include hefty administrative surcharges (up to 40% markup).
Toll Systems (Salik / Darb) Automated transponder passes billed at AED 4-5 per gate. Tolls are settled electronically upon return. Retain receipts to reconcile your final statement against toll logs.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can tourists drive in Saudi Arabia and the UAE with a foreign license?
Yes. Visitors on tourist or visit visas can drive rental cars using a recognized foreign license accompanied by an International Driving Permit (IDP). Citizens of GCC nations (Saudi Arabia, UAE, Kuwait, Qatar, Bahrain, Oman) can drive using their national domestic license without an IDP. Expatriates who transition to an Iqama or residency visa must convert to a local driver's license.
How much security deposit is held for rental cars in the GCC?
Security deposits typically range from SAR/AED 1,000 to 3,500 depending on the vehicle class (Economy vs SUV/Luxury). Deposits are placed as a temporary credit card pre-authorization and released within 14 to 28 days following vehicle return to account for potential traffic fines (Saher / Dubai Police) or automated toll charges (Salik/Darb).
Is a private airport transfer better than taking an on-arrival taxi?
Private airport transfers provide a guaranteed fixed upfront rate, flight delay monitoring, and a driver meet-and-greet in the arrivals hall with personalized signage. On-arrival metered taxis are convenient for solo travelers but can incur airport starting surcharges, night rates, and traffic wait fees, especially during peak pilgrimage or conference seasons.
What is the minimum age to rent a car in Saudi Arabia and the UAE?
The minimum legal age to rent a car across most rental agencies in Saudi Arabia and the UAE is 21 years old with at least 1 year of valid driving experience. Drivers between 21 and 24 often incur a young driver daily surcharge and may have restricted access to luxury or high-performance SUV categories.
How do road tolls work for rental cars in Dubai and Abu Dhabi?
All rental vehicles in the UAE are equipped with automated Salik (Dubai) and Darb (Abu Dhabi) toll tags. Each time you pass an automated toll gate, an electronic charge (typically AED 4 to 5 per gate plus a standard 15% agency administrative fee) is recorded and billed against your deposit upon return.
